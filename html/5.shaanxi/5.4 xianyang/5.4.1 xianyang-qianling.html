Qianling Mausoleum Overview

A comprehensive guide to Qianling Mausoleum, the joint tomb of Emperor Gaozong and Empress Wu Zetian — the only Tang imperial tomb never looted, and home to China's most enigmatic ancient stele.

Qianling Mausoleum (乾陵), carved into the granite peak of Liangshan Mountain 1,047 meters above sea level, is the final resting place of Emperor Gaozong of Tang and his wife Empress Wu Zetian — China's only female emperor, and the only woman in history to be buried in an imperial tomb of her own rank. No other Chinese mausoleum holds two emperors. More remarkably, Qianling has never been looted: every attempt by grave-robbers, peasant rebels, and even a warlord's army armed with dynamite has failed to breach the 1,300-year-old seal, which is thought to survive intact beneath the granite.

Surrounding the main tomb are 17 satellite tombs containing royal family members and high-ranking officials. Three are open to visitors: the tombs of Princess Yongtai, Prince Yide and Prince Zhanghuai. The site is also famed for its Uncharactered Stele — a silent monolith standing 6.3 meters tall that Empress Wu had erected to memorialize herself, deliberately left blank so that "merits and demerits may be judged by posterity." Along the Sacred Way leading to the mausoleum stand 120 stone figures, including the mysterious Sixty-One Foreign Envoys — headless stone statues representing dignitaries from the Tang Empire's vassal states, their severed heads a centuries-old puzzle.

Recommended Route

Qianling Mausoleum follows a one-way route served entirely by sightseeing shuttles that connect each stop in sequence. There is no return path — once you finish one stop, you board the next shuttle forward. The recommended order below is fixed, and each stop rewards patience.

  • Stop 1 — Princess Yongtai's Tomb (乾陵博物馆 / 永泰公主墓) — Entrance & Museum: This is where the sightseeing shuttle departs. Princess Yongtai's actual tomb may be closed for restoration, but the adjacent Qianling Museum is well worth your first hour. It houses a superb collection of excavated artifacts — gold and silver vessels, jade carvings, and exquisite mural reproductions that bring Tang Dynasty court life to vivid detail.
  • Stop 2 — Prince Yide & Prince Zhanghuai's Tombs (懿德太子墓 / 章怀太子墓) — Descend into Tang Tombs: The shuttle passes both satellite tombs in succession. You can descend into the burial corridors of both — a rare chance to walk inside intact Tang Dynasty tomb structures. Inside Prince Zhanghuai's tomb, look for the famous "Polo Match" mural (马球图), and in Prince Yide's tomb, the ceiling dome murals are the star.
  • Stop 3 — Sacred Way & Uncharactered Stele (神道与无字碑) — The Core of Qianling: This is the heart of the mausoleum. Walk the long Sacred Way flanked by colossal stone figures — hailed as an "open-air museum of high-Tang stone sculpture" — and visit two unmissable landmarks:
    — Uncharactered Stele (无字碑): Wu Zetian's 6.3-meter-tall blank stele, erected deliberately without text so that history alone would judge her reign. Standing before its silent granite bulk, you feel the full weight of 1,300 years of debate.
    — Sixty-One Foreign Envoys (六十一蕃臣像): Headless stone statues of dignitaries from the Tang Empire's vassal states, a powerful testimony to the cosmopolitan reach of the Tang court — whose severed heads remain one of ancient China's great archaeological mysteries.

Detailed Information

Location

G312 National Highway, Chengguan Town, Qian County, Xianyang, Shaanxi Province

Opening Hours

Peak Season（March–November）: 8:00-18:00

Off-Peak Season（December–February）: 8:30-17:00

Best Time to Visit

March–November (spring cherry blossoms on Liangshan; autumn golden light on the Sacred Way)

Tickets

¥100/person (Peak Season)

¥80/person (Off-Peak Season)

Reservation Information

Recommended on weekends; walk-ins available midweek

Transportation

Drive from Xi'an (1.5 h) / Xianyang (1 h); or take bus from Qian County
