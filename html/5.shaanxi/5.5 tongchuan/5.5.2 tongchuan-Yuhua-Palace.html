Yuhua Palace Overview

A comprehensive guide to Yuhua Palace (玉华宫) — the Tang Dynasty royal summer palace where the revered monk Xuanzang spent his final years translating Buddhist sutras, set within a forested highland often called a "natural oxygen bar."

A Tang Dynasty Royal Summer Palace. First commissioned in 647 AD (Zhenguan 21) by Emperor Gaozu (Li Yuan) as a summer retreat and originally named "Renzhi Palace" (仁智宫), it was later expanded and renamed "Yuhua Palace" (玉华宫) by Emperor Taizong (Li Shimin). So grand was its scale that it was celebrated as one of the "Four Great Detached Palaces of the Tang Dynasty" — alongside Jiucheng Palace, Huaqing Palace, and Cuiwei Palace. The Tang poets Du Fu and Cen Shen once composed verses praising its magnificent scenery.

Xuanzang's Final Chapter. In 651 AD (Yonghui 2), under Emperor Gaozong, the palace was converted into the Buddhist monastery "Yuhua Temple" (玉华寺). In 659 AD (Xianqing 4), the revered monk Xuanzang was summoned here by imperial decree to translate Buddhist sutras, remaining until his parinirvana at Yuhua Temple in 664 AD (Linde 1). This made Yuhua the site of Xuanzang's late-life translation work and final passing — endowing it with profound significance in the history of Chinese Buddhism.

From Ruin to Revival. After the An Lushan Rebellion the palace-monastery gradually fell into ruin, and by the Qing Dynasty only traces remained. Since the 1980s, the government has undertaken archaeological excavation and conservation restoration, reviving this historic site for visitors today — a place where Tang imperial ambition and Buddhist devotion still whisper through the pines.

Must-See Highlights

Yuhua Palace unfolds across forested hills and Tang-era ruins. Walk the palace foundations, step into the cave where Xuanzang translated sutras, and cool off beneath a near-total forest canopy. The four must-see spots below span history, faith, and nature — all reachable on foot within the scenic area.

  • 1. Xuanzang Memorial Hall (玄奘纪念馆) — The Translator's Legacy: Displays artifacts, documents, and Silk Road cultural-exchange materials chronicling Xuanzang's sutra-translation work at Yuhua Temple — the best place to grasp why this remote palace matters to the history of Chinese Buddhism.
  • 2. Heritage Protection Zone (遗址保护区) — Where Xuanzang Lived and Worked: Includes Tang Dynasty palace foundations, grottoes (the Sucheng Courtyard site 肃成院遗址), and Buddhist niches. Sucheng Courtyard is the core area where Xuanzang lived and translated sutras — the spiritual heart of the entire site.
  • 3. Yuhua Palace Forest Park (玉华宫森林公园) — A Natural Oxygen Bar: The surrounding forest coverage reaches 95%, with cool summers (average ~23°C) — earning the name "natural oxygen bar." Ideal for summer retreat, hiking, and forest bathing beneath dense pine and broadleaf canopy.
  • 4. Grottoes & Buddhist Relics (石窟与佛教遗存) — Faith Carved in Stone: Tang Dynasty cliff carvings and remnant Buddhist sculptures survive here, reflecting the fusion of Buddhist art and imperial architecture — quiet stone witnesses to a thousand years of devotion.

Opening Hours

Peak season: 8:00-18:00

Off-season: 8:30-17:00

Best Time to Visit

May-October (summer cool retreat, avg. ~23°C); autumn for golden forests.
