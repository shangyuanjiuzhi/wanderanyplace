Li Zicheng's Palace Overview

A comprehensive guide to Li Zicheng's Temporary Palace, a landmark of peasant revolution history.

Li Zicheng's Temporary Palace (李自成行宫) was built for Li Zicheng, the rebel leader who overthrew the Ming Dynasty and briefly founded the Shun Dynasty in 1644. Perched on a hill in his hometown of Mizhi County, the complex features grand halls, imperial architecture, and exhibits about the life and legacy of one of China's most famous peasant revolutionaries.

Li Zicheng, known as the 'Dashing King' (闯王), led a massive peasant rebellion that culminated in the fall of Beijing and the end of the Ming Dynasty. The palace in Mizhi was constructed to celebrate his victory and honor his family. Today, it stands as a historical landmark commemorating one of the most significant events in Chinese history.

Photo Recommendations

  • Panlong Mountain Distant View (盘龙山远眺): Use a wide-angle lens from the square in front of the palace to capture the full architectural grandeur — evoking the majesty of a soaring dragon.
  • Jade Emperor Pavilion (玉皇阁): As the highest point of the palace, it offers panoramic views of Mizhi County and the Wuding River in the distance — an exceptional vantage point.
  • Bagua Pavilion (八卦亭 / 捧圣楼): From here you can photograph the cave-dwelling clusters of old Mizhi, rich in cultural and human atmosphere.
  • Music Tower (乐楼): Use light and shadow to capture the exquisitely carved doors and windows — brimming with historical charm.
