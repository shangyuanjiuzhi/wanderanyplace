Baoji Bronze Ware Museum Overview

A guide to the Baoji Bronze Ware Museum (宝鸡青铜器博物院) — the largest bronze-ware museum in China, standing on the very soil where the Zhou and Qin civilizations were born.

Baoji is the cradle of the Western Zhou and Qin states — and the earth here has yielded one of the world's richest hoards of ancient bronze. The museum, housed in the China Stone Drum Park, gathers over 4,870 bronze relics spanning the Shang, Western Zhou, and Spring-and-Autumn periods, tracing the ritual heart of early Chinese statehood.

Its three greatest treasures — the He Zun, the Lai Pan, and the Hu Gui — are national-treasure-grade artifacts that re-wrote Chinese historiography. Free to enter, the museum is an essential stop for anyone tracing the very origins of the word "China."

Star Exhibits

The museum's three national-treasure-grade bronzes each re-wrote a chapter of early Chinese history — from the origin of the word "China," to a bronze "history book," to the largest surviving ceremonial gui. Together they trace the ritual and political heart of the Western Zhou.

  • 1. He Zun (何尊) — The Origin of "China": The inscription on its inner base bears the phrase "宅兹中国" (zhái zī zhōng guó) — the earliest known written occurrence of the word "China" (中国), set down some three thousand years ago. "Why China? Look here for the source" — a single glance traces the concept of the Central Kingdom back to its very origin.
  • 2. Lai Pan (逨盘) — A Bronze History Book: A bronze basin whose 372-character inscription is, in effect, a bronze "history book." It fully corroborates the succession of the Western Zhou kings and, cross-checked against the Records of the Grand Historian (史记), resolves historiographical puzzles that had stood for a thousand years.
  • 3. Hu Gui (㝬簋) — The King of Gui: The largest surviving bronze gui vessel — a heavy ceremonial piece cast for the personal use of King Li of Zhou (周厉王). Its imposing, solemn form embodies the regal grandeur and ritual authority of the Western Zhou court.

Detailed Information

Location

China Stone Drum Park (中华石鼓园), Binhe Avenue, Baoji, Shaanxi Province

Opening Hours

9:00-17:00 (Tue-Sun, closed Mondays)

Best Time to Visit

All year round — an indoor museum, ideal in summer heat or winter cold.

Tickets

Free entry (valid ID or passport required)

Reservation Information

Free ticket issued with ID at the gate; group visits by advance appointment

Transportation

City bus within Baoji to Stone Drum Park; high-speed rail to Baoji South Station, then a short taxi ride
