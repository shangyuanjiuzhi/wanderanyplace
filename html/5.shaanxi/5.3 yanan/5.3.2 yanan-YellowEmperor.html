Mausoleum Overview

A comprehensive guide to the Yellow Emperor's Mausoleum — the most sacred ancestral site of the Chinese civilization, known as "the First Mausoleum Under Heaven."

The Yellow Emperor's Mausoleum (黄帝陵), nestled among 80,000 ancient cypresses on Qiaoshan Mountain in Huangling County, is the legendary burial site of Huangdi — the mythological ancestor of the Chinese nation. Revered for over two millennia, it has drawn imperial pilgrims from every dynasty and remains the venue for China's annual Qingming Grand Ancestral Worship Ceremony. The entire scenic area is divided into two parts: the Xuanyuan Temple on the mountainside and the Mausoleum itself at the summit, with a recommended visit duration of 3–4 hours.

The mountain is cloaked in a vast ancient cypress forest — some trees over a thousand years old — creating a solemn, verdant atmosphere that has made this site a place of pilgrimage for emperors, scholars, and modern visitors alike. In 1961, it was designated a National Key Cultural Relic Protection Unit, and in 2006, the Qingming Ancestral Worship Ceremony was listed as a National Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Tour Route & Key Highlights

The scenic area is divided into two zones: the Xuanyuan Temple on the mountainside (first stop) and the Mausoleum at the summit (second stop). The images on the right show the six key landmarks along the route.

  • Stop 1 · Xuanyuan Temple (轩辕庙) — The Worship Hall: The primary venue for honoring the Yellow Emperor and the highlight of the lower area. Hiring a guide (approx. ¥55 per group) is highly recommended for a deeper understanding of the history and symbolism.
  • Hand-Planted Cypress (黄帝手植柏) — "Father of World Cypresses": The most awe-inspiring sight in the temple grounds. Legend holds that the Yellow Emperor himself planted this tree some 5,000 years ago. Its massive trunk requires several people to encircle, and it is revered as the world's oldest cultivated cypress.
  • Stele Pavilion & Corridor (碑亭与碑廊) — Inscriptions of the Ages: Preserves a remarkable collection of steles bearing sacrificial texts and calligraphy from successive dynasties, including modern leaders such as Sun Yat-sen, Chiang Kai-shek, Mao Zedong, and Deng Xiaoping — a deeply historical corridor bridging millennia.
  • Xuanyuan Hall (轩辕殿) — "Round Heaven, Square Earth": A modern grand ceremonial hall built on the ancient cosmological principle of a circular roof above a square base. Magnificent in scale, it is the venue for the annual Qingming Grand Ancestral Worship Ceremony, attended by dignitaries from across China and overseas.
  • Stop 2 · Hanwu Immortal Platform (汉武仙台) — Emperor Wu's Vista: After exiting the temple, take the shuttle bus (¥20 round trip) or walk 20–40 minutes uphill. This elevated platform was built by Emperor Wu of the Han Dynasty when he came to worship the Yellow Emperor. Climb to the top for a sweeping overlook of the ancient cypress sea blanketing Qiaoshan.
  • Mausoleum Mound (黄帝陵冢) — The Heart of the Site: The core of the entire scenic area — a simple, unadorned earth mound said to be the clothing tomb (衣冠冢) of the Yellow Emperor. Visitors traditionally circle the mound three times clockwise to pay respects and offer prayers for blessings and good fortune.
  • Longyu Pavilion (龙驭阁) — Panorama of Qiaoshan: Perched at the highest point of the mausoleum zone, this pavilion offers the finest panoramic view. Climb to the top to take in the full sweep of Qiaoshan Mountain — rolling cypress ridges, the winding Ju River valley, and the loess plateau stretching to the horizon.
