Niubeiliang Overview

A comprehensive guide to Niubeiliang National Forest Park (牛背梁国家森林公园) — the highest peak of the eastern Qinling at 2,802 m, a vast alpine preserve with old-growth oak forests, spruce groves, wild azalea meadows, and one of China's best-preserved habitats of the golden takin.

Niubeiliang, at 2,802 m above sea level, is the highest peak of the eastern Qinling Mountains — a vast alpine preserve of old-growth oak forests, spruce groves, wild azalea meadows, and verdant alpine tundra. Named for the ridgeline that resembles the hump of an ox (niu bei), it is also one of the last and best-preserved habitats of the golden takin (羚牛), a rare goat-antelope native to the Qinling.

The park offers two distinct hiking routes: the easy Takun Valley plank walk for casual visitors, and the challenging Camel Peak alpine hike for fit adventurers. A cable car connects the valley to the mid-elevation zone, making the high scenery accessible to most. Designated nature-walk zones are guided by rangers for wildlife spotting.

Route 1 — Easy Plank-Walk Route

This is the default choice for most visitors. The full route is about 3.7 km along a gentle plank walk — flat, shaded, and cool even in midsummer. Streams tumble beside the path, waterfalls cascade into plunge pools, and dense oak and spruce canopy blocks the sun. It is literally a natural "forest air-conditioner." Children and seniors can walk it easily, and there are plenty of spots to stop, splash in the water, and breathe in the mountain air.

Highlights: Takun Valley stream, waterfalls, dense forest, golden-takin viewing platforms

Route 2 — Deep Trekking Route

For fit hikers, this route adds the alpine layer. After the Takun Valley plank walk, take the cable car up to the mid-elevation zone, then hike the Camel Peak (骆驼峰) ridgeline. The trail is steeper and more exposed, but rewards with wild azalea meadows, panoramic views of the eastern Qinling, and the dramatic ox-hump ridgeline that gave Niubeiliang its name. Hike down on a different trail, then catch a shuttle bus back to the visitor center.

Highlights: Camel Peak ridgeline, alpine meadows, azalea blooms (June-July), 2,802 m summit

Park Highlights

Niubeiliang pairs a cool, accessible valley walk with a challenging alpine hike — two very different mountain experiences in one park. The Takun Valley section is Shaanxi's best summer escape for families, while Camel Peak delivers the raw Qinling beauty that serious hikers crave.

  • 1. Takun Valley (羚牛谷) — Cool Forest Plank Walk: A 3.7 km flat plank walk beside a tumbling stream, with waterfalls, plunge pools, and old-growth forest. Natural air conditioning in July and August — the most popular section for casual visitors and families.
  • 2. Camel Peak (骆驼峰) — Alpine Ridge Adventure: Accessed by cable car, this ridgeline hike reaches 2,802 m — the highest point in the eastern Qinling. Wild azalea meadows bloom in June-July; the rest of the year, expect panoramic mountain views and crisp alpine air.
  • 3. Golden Takin Habitat (羚牛保护区) — Wildlife Spotting: Niubeiliang is one of China's last strongholds of the golden takin, a rare goat-antelope endemic to the Qinling. Guided ranger walks depart from the Takun Valley service area — early morning is the best time for sightings.
