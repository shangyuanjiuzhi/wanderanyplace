Bund Overview

A comprehensive guide to Shanghai's most iconic waterfront, the Bund.

The Bund is Shanghai's most famous waterfront promenade, stretching along the western bank of the Huangpu River. Lined with 52 historic buildings from the late 19th and early 20th centuries, it represents a collection of architectural styles including Gothic, Baroque, Renaissance, and Art Deco. These buildings were once the headquarters of foreign banks, trading companies, and consulates, earning the Bund the nickname "Wall Street of the East."

Today, the Bund is a major tourist attraction and symbol of Shanghai's history and modernity. It offers stunning views of the Pudong skyline across the river, featuring iconic landmarks like the Oriental Pearl Tower, Shanghai Tower, and Jin Mao Tower. The Bund is also a popular spot for evening walks when the historic buildings are beautifully illuminated.

Recommended Tour Route

The recommended route for visiting the Bund starts from Customs House in the south and extends north to Suzhou Creek. This 1.5km walk takes approximately 2-3 hours, allowing you to admire all 52 historic buildings. The best time to visit is during golden hour (1 hour before sunset) when the lighting is perfect for photography, transitioning into the spectacular evening light show.

Key highlights include the Peace Hotel, HSBC Building, and Customs House. For a unique perspective, take a Huangpu River cruise to view the Bund from the water, or cross to Pudong for panoramic photos of the historic skyline.

Must-see Landmarks

Peace Hotel

The Peace Hotel is one of Shanghai's most iconic landmarks, known for its distinctive green pyramid roof and Art Deco architecture. Built in 1929, it was originally called the Cathay Hotel and was once the tallest building in Asia. The hotel has hosted many famous guests including Charlie Chaplin, Noël Coward, and George Bernard Shaw.

Today, the Peace Hotel is a luxury hotel that preserves much of its original Art Deco interior, including the famous Jazz Bar where a band has been playing since the 1930s. The hotel's rooftop offers stunning views of the Bund and Pudong skyline.

10:00 - 22:00 20 Nanjing Road East Free entrance (hotel guests)

HSBC Building

The HSBC Building, also known as the Shanghai Pudong Development Bank Building, is a masterpiece of neoclassical architecture. Built in 1923, it was once the headquarters of the Hong Kong and Shanghai Banking Corporation and was considered the "most luxurious building east of the Suez Canal."

The building features a grand dome with intricate mosaics depicting scenes from around the world, and its facade is adorned with columns and sculptures. The entrance hall contains two large bronze lions that have become iconic symbols of Shanghai.

9:00 - 17:00 60m NW of Zhongshan East 1st Rd & Fuzhou Rd Intersection, Huangpu District Free (limited access)

Customs House

The Customs House is famous for its clock tower, which features the largest clock face in Asia. Built in 1927, the building combines classical and modern architectural styles. The clock tower stands at 83.2 meters tall and has been a landmark of the Bund for nearly a century.

The clock mechanism was made by the same company that made Big Ben in London, and it still chimes every hour. The building now houses the Shanghai Customs Museum, which showcases the history of Shanghai's customs service.

9:00 - 16:30 13 Zhongshan East Road Free

Waibaidu Bridge

Waibaidu Bridge is the oldest steel bridge in Shanghai and an iconic landmark connecting the Bund with Hongkou District. Built in 1907, it was the first bridge across the Suzhou Creek and has appeared in numerous films and television shows.

The bridge offers stunning views of the Bund skyline and is a popular spot for photographers, especially at night when it is beautifully lit. Walking across the bridge provides a unique perspective of both the historic Bund and the modern city.

24 hours 111 North Suzhou Road, Hongkou District Free

Free Buildings to Visit

Overview

This walking route covers 13 historic buildings and museums along the Bund that are free to visit, offering a comprehensive journey through Shanghai's colonial-era history. However, please note that the Customs House is currently not accessible to visitors without special exhibitions.

The map and free buildings on the right shows the recommended walking route and free buildings to visit.

1. Shanghai Postal Museum

Housed in the former Shanghai Post Office building completed in 1924, this Beaux-Arts landmark showcases postal history from the 19th century to today. Exhibits include rare stamps, vintage mailboxes, and uniforms. The rooftop offers panoramic views of Suzhou Creek.

2. Shanghai Securities Museum

Located in the former Shanghai Stock Exchange building, this Art Deco museum traces the evolution of China's securities market since the late 19th century. It features historical trading floors, stock certificates, and interactive displays about Shanghai's financial development.

3. The Bund Source No. 1

Built in 1849 as the British Consulate General, this site marks the birthplace of the Bund. Today it's a cultural complex preserving colonial architecture, with beautifully landscaped gardens and art galleries reflecting Shanghai's foreign concessions era.

4. The Bund History Museum

This museum chronicles the Bund's transformation from a muddy riverbank to the "Wall Street of the East." Through historical photos, architectural plans, and interactive exhibits, visitors gain insight into the waterfront's 150-year development.

5. Peace Hotel

Originally the Cathay Hotel (1929), this iconic Art Deco landmark was Asia's most luxurious hotel. It hosted celebrities including Charlie Chaplin and Noël Coward. The famous Jazz Bar and marble-floored lobby remain architectural highlights.

6. Former Chartered Bank Building

Completed in 1923 for the Chartered Bank of India, Australia and China, this neoclassical building features a distinctive copper dome. It was one of the most important foreign bank headquarters on the Bund during the colonial period.

7. Customs House

Finished in 1927, this Greek Revival landmark features a clock tower modeled after London's Big Ben, playing the Westminster Quarters every 15 minutes. Currently closed to visitors without special exhibitions.

8. Former HSBC Bank Building

Described as "the finest building between Suez and Bering Strait," this 1923 neoclassical masterpiece features a magnificent dome. Now housing Shanghai Pudong Development Bank, its grand lobby with marble floors remains open to the public.

9. Former Shanghai Club

Built in 1910 as a social club for British expatriates, this elegant building features a distinctive veranda with Ionic columns. The famous "longest bar in Asia" once stretched 34 meters. Now part of the Peninsula Hotel.

10. Former Asiatic Petroleum Building

Completed in 1916 as the headquarters of Asiatic Petroleum Company, this Renaissance Revival building occupies a prominent corner site. Its classical columns and decorative details reflect Shanghai's early 20th-century oil industry prosperity.

11. Shanghai Telecommunications Museum

Housed in the former Great Northern Telegraph Company building (1922), this museum traces Shanghai's communication evolution from telegraphs to digital age. Exhibits include vintage telephones, switchboards, and rare equipment.

12. Bank of China Building

Completed in 1937 as Shanghai's tallest building, this modern Chinese landmark combines pagoda-like roof with Western construction. Its interior features traditional Chinese motifs, and the ground floor lobby remains accessible to visitors.

13. Former Butterfield & Swire Building

Originally the Taikoo Trading Company headquarters, this historical commercial building reflects the presence of major British trading firms in 20th-century Shanghai. Its architecture represents the Bund's commercial prosperity era.

Various hours (check individual listings) Zhongshan East Road area Free
