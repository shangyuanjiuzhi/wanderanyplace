Panda Superstars: The Pandas Chinese Fans Queue For

One sits like a triangular rice ball and answers to a Sichuan-accented nickname. One was born on Olympics opening day. One spent his early years as a "female" until a DNA test. Meet the headliners.

Introduction

In China, pandas are not anonymous. They have names, nicknames, fan groups, merchandise and rivalries. The most popular ones are called 顶流 — "top-tier traffic", the same term used for A-list celebrities — and their keepers are recognised on the street.

Some pandas are famous for their shape, some for a viral clip, and at least one for a biological surprise that made national news. This guide introduces the three headliners and the rest of the star map, with the story behind each name and the base where you can actually see them.

One warning before you plan: pandas move between bases, and a star can be resting, relocated or off display on any given day. Treat the locations below as the usual arrangement and check the base's official update before you travel.

How a Panda Becomes a Star

Every panda born in captivity gets an official name, usually a combination of the mother's name and a character chosen by the base. Fame works differently. Three ingredients tend to decide which pandas break out of the enclosure and into the feed.

01

A silhouette you can draw

Hua Hua's round body and short legs make her look like a triangular rice ball when she sits. A shape is easier to meme than a face.

02

A catchphrase

A keeper calling in local dialect — "花花，果赖" — gives the internet a line it can repeat, remix and attach to a personality.

03

A story with a twist

Mei Lan's gender reveal had everything: a beautiful panda, a failed matchmaking event, DNA, and a nickname that stuck for fifteen years.

The short version

If you have one morning and want the biggest star, go to the Chengdu base's Villa 6 for Hua Hua and He Ye, entering from the South Gate. If you want a quieter panda day, Panda Valley has Aoliao and the "dance queen" Mei Lan. Wolong is where Xiao Qiji and Fu Bao draw their fans.

1. Hua Hua (成和花): The Absolute Centre

Hua Hua is the undisputed C-position of the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding. Born in 2020, she is small, round and short-legged, and when she sits down her outline becomes an isosceles triangle — a 三角饭团, or triangular rice ball, a comparison that spread far beyond panda fans.

Her personality is the other half of the appeal. Hua Hua is gentle, slow and completely unbothered. She does not fight for food, often has her bamboo taken by other pandas, and reacts to almost everything with the same calm expression. Chinese fans call it 佛系 — a Buddhist indifference to the chaos around her — and that calmness is what people find soothing.

The clip that made her a national name involves her keeper, known to fans as Grandpa Tan (谭爷爷). He calls across the enclosure in Sichuan dialect — "花花，果赖" — 果赖 being the local pronunciation of 过来, "come here". Hua Hua lifts her head and walks over slowly, in exactly the unhurried way her fans expect. The clip has been remixed thousands of times.

Where to see Hua Hua

Hua Hua and her younger sister He Ye (和叶) are usually in Villa 6 (六号别墅) in the old area of the Chengdu base. Enter from the South Gate (南门) — it is the closest gate. Go at opening time; by late morning the crowd is several rows deep. Hua Hua rests on Mondays, so avoid that day if she is the reason you came.

2. Aoliao (奥莉奥 / 奥利奥): The Olympic Baby

Aoliao's origin story is built into her name. She was born on 28 July 2012, the day of the London Olympics opening ceremony, and immediately became the base's "Olympic baby" (奥运宝宝). Her mother is Lili (莉莉), and the base ran a global naming contest to mark the coincidence.

The winning name, 奥莉奥, combines 奥 from 奥运 (Olympics) with 莉 from her mother's name — a neat two-character biography. Fans also write it as 奥利奥, and call her Oreo, because the black-and-white patches around her face look like the biscuit. Search either spelling and you will find the same panda.

Aoliao is now associated with Dujiangyan Panda Valley, the semi-wild base in the hills outside Chengdu. That makes her an easy add-on to a Dujiangyan day: pandas in the morning, the irrigation system or Qingcheng Mountain in the afternoon.

3. Mei Lan (美兰): The Gender Reveal

Mei Lan's story is the best-known panda plot twist in China. Born in 2006 at Zoo Atlanta, he had a sweet face and a gentle temperament, so the world assumed he was female. His name, 美兰, was read as "the beauty of Atlanta", and for the first years of his life every sign pointed that way.

In 2010 he moved to the Chengdu base. The base organised a grand matchmaking event for him, inviting eight of the handsomest young males at the time. Mei Lan showed no interest in any of them — and then became aggressive. Keepers ran a DNA test. The "beauty" was male.

The internet responded with the sort of nickname that never dies: 大姐, "Big Sister". One of the unsuccessful suitors, a panda called Wuyi (五一), was immortalised in the joke "一见美兰误终身" — one look at Mei Lan and your life is ruined.

Mei Lan is male, and he remains one of the base's most recognisable characters. Do not confuse him with 梅兰 (different character, same English spelling), the Panda Valley panda below.

4. The Rest of the Star Map

Beyond the three headliners, a handful of pandas have their own fan clubs. These are the ones most likely to appear in your feed — and in the enclosure next to the one you came to see.

He Ye · 和叶

Chengdu base · Villa 6 area

Hua Hua's younger sister, and her exact opposite: fast, loud and food-greedy. She climbs on Hua Hua, steals her bamboo and generally behaves like a small bandit.

Mei Lan · 梅兰

Dujiangyan Panda Valley

Not the Atlanta Mei Lan. This 梅兰 is famous for moving with unusual style — fans call her the "Dance Queen of the Panda World" (舞林萌主).

Xiao Qiji · 小奇迹

Wolong Shenshuping

A popular panda at the mountain base in Wolong, and one of the reasons fans are willing to make the long drive.

Fu Bao · 福宝

Wolong Shenshuping

Born in Korea, Fu Bao returned to China with a huge existing fan base and was briefly shown at Shenshuping, drawing crowds that rivalled the city base.

6. Panda Etiquette (Especially at a Star's Enclosure)

  • Do not call the panda by name. Keepers can, because the panda knows them. A crowd shouting "花花" only adds stress.
  • No flash, no tapping on glass, no food. Pandas have sensitive ears and strict diets.
  • Keep moving at busy enclosures. A few minutes is usually enough; the queue behind you is not.
  • Take photos from the marked line. Staff will move you on if you block the path or climb the barrier.
  • Check the daily update. Bases post which pandas are on display; a star can be resting or relocated without warning.
  • Buy tickets with your passport. Carry the same document to the gate, and book ahead on weekends and holidays.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Hua Hua so famous?

Three things came together: her round body and short legs make her sit like an isosceles triangular rice ball; her calm, uncompetitive personality is genuinely soothing to watch; and a clip of her keeper calling “花花，果赖” in Sichuan dialect, with Hua Hua slowly walking over, spread across Chinese social media.

Where can I see Hua Hua?

At the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding, mainly in Villa 6 (六号别墅) in the old area, together with her younger sister He Ye. Enter from the South Gate (南门), which is closest. Go at opening time, and avoid Monday if Hua Hua is the main reason for your visit.

Is Hua Hua male or female?

Hua Hua, whose full name is 成和花, is female. She was born in 2020 at the Chengdu base and is one of the most recognisable pandas in China.

Who is He Ye?

He Ye (和叶) is Hua Hua's younger sister and lives in the same area. Her personality is the opposite of Hua Hua's: fast, playful and food-greedy, often climbing on her sister or stealing her bamboo. Fans call her a little bandit.

Why is Aoliao called the Olympic baby?

Aoliao (奥莉奥, also written 奥利奥) was born on 28 July 2012, the day of the London Olympics opening ceremony. Her mother is Lili (莉莉), and her name was chosen in a global naming contest: “奥” for the Olympics, “莉” from her mother.

Is Mei Lan really male?

Yes. Mei Lan (美兰) was born at Zoo Atlanta in 2006 and was raised as a female because of his sweet face and gentle temperament. After he returned to Chengdu in 2010, a DNA test during a failed matchmaking event confirmed he was male. Fans still call him “大姐” (Big Sister) as a joke.

Is Mei Lan the same panda as Mei Lan at Panda Valley?

No. 美兰 (Mei Lan, the Atlanta-born male) and 梅兰 (Mei Lan, the Panda Valley “dance queen”) are different pandas with different Chinese characters. This article keeps the characters next to each name so you can tell them apart.

Do I need to queue to see the famous pandas?

On weekends and Chinese holidays, yes. Arrive before opening, go straight to the panda you most want to see, and expect a short, managed viewing time at the busiest enclosures. Weekday mornings are calmer and the pandas are more active.

Conclusion

What makes a panda a star is not the panda itself but the story people tell about it: a triangular silhouette, a keeper's call, a failed matchmaking event, a nickname that survived fifteen years. Knowing the stories changes the experience — the enclosure stops being a pen of bamboo and becomes a character you have been following. Go early, stay quiet, and let the panda be a panda.
