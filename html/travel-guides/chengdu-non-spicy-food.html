Not Spicy Chengdu: 8 Dishes That Won't Burn Your Mouth

Chengdu's reputation is chilli oil, but locals eat plenty of food with no heat at all: milky pig-trotter soup, clear-broth beef, syrup-glazed duck, sweet noodles and iced jelly.

Introduction

Say "Chengdu food" and most people picture a red cauldron of beef tallow and chillies. That picture is real, but it is not the whole table. Locals start the day with a sweet egg waffle, eat clear-soup wontons at lunch, snack on syrup-glazed duck in the afternoon and cool down with a bowl of iced jelly at night.

Sichuan cooking is built on more than twenty flavour profiles, and only a handful are chilli-forward. Clear broth, sweet-savoury glazes, sesame, peanut and fermented rice do just as much work. If you cannot eat spice — or simply need a break from it — Chengdu is easier than its reputation suggests.

Below are eight dishes that are safe for a no-chilli trip, each with a heat rating, what it tastes like and the words you need to order it.

Heat key

0/5 no chilli at all · 1/5 a hint you can barely taste · 2/5 mild but noticeable. Everything on this list is 0 or 1.

Why Not Everything in Chengdu Is Hot

The chilli arrived in Sichuan only in the late Qing dynasty, carried in from the coast. The region already had a sophisticated kitchen built on clear broths, tea-smoking, sweet-savoury sauces and the famous "strange flavour" (怪味) balance of sweet, sour, salty, numbing and nutty. Chilli became one voice in the choir, not the whole choir.

The practical result is a menu where heat is optional. Restaurants cook to order, so a dish listed as spicy can usually be made mild, and a clear-soup version often exists next to the red-oil one. The eight dishes below are the easiest places to start.

1. Mom's Pig-Trotter Soup (老妈蹄花)

Heat 0/5 · Not spicy

A bowl of milky white soup with a whole pig's trotter simmered until it collapses. It is the classic Chengdu late-night dish, and the one locals recommend first when someone says they cannot eat chilli.

  • Not spicy at all. The trotter is stewed in clear water with ginger and a little white pepper, so the broth is mild and milky. The dipping sauce alongside is fragrant rather than hot.
  • Falls off the bone. After hours of simmering the meat is soft enough that chopsticks separate bone from skin. The texture is gelatinous and melts on the tongue.
  • Nourishing broth. The white colour comes from collagen, not cream. Locals treat it as a restorative dish — good after a long flight, a cold night or too much hotpot.

2. Tiaojiao Beef (跷脚牛肉)

Heat 0/5 · Not spicy

A Leshan dish that migrated to Chengdu: thin slices of beef and offal cooked in a clear, slightly medicinal broth. The name means "one foot up" — porters used to eat it at street stalls with one foot resting on a stool.

  • Clear broth, no chilli. The base is a light stock with ginger and a few herbs. It tastes of beef rather than of spice, and is the first choice for a mild meal.
  • Tender meat, clear soup. Beef and offal are sliced paper-thin and dipped into the hot broth, so they cook in seconds and stay soft. The soup itself is bright and clean.
  • Dipping sauce is your choice. The default saucer is not spicy. If you want heat, add chilli oil yourself; if you do not, eat it with the broth and a little garlic.

3. Sweet-Skin Duck (甜皮鸭)

Heat 0/5 · Sweet and savoury

A Leshan-style duck with a glossy layer of maltose syrup over crisp skin. It is sold by weight, chopped into pieces, and wrapped in paper — the most portable non-spicy food in Sichuan.

  • Sweet, savoury, tender. The skin is crisp and sweet, the meat is savoury and soft, and the overall balance is sweet-salty rather than cloying. There is no chilli in the glaze.
  • The best chilli reset. Keep a bag in your hotel room. One piece between hotpot meals resets your mouth and settles the burn better than water.
  • Easy to take away. Buy a quarter or half duck, ask for it chopped (切块), and eat it within a day. It travels well on trains and flights.

4. Egg Waffle (蛋烘糕)

Heat 0/5 · Sweet or savoury

A small, bowl-shaped pancake cooked in a copper mould, folded around a filling and eaten from a paper bag. It is Chengdu's most common street snack and almost never contains chilli.

  • Sweet or savoury fillings. Cream and pork floss is the most popular combination; red bean, peanut butter, jam and savoury minced meat also appear. All are chilli-free.
  • Crisp outside, soft inside. The edge sets crisp while the centre stays spongy. It is cheap — usually a few yuan — and the filling is generous for the price.
  • Avoid the queue. The famous stalls can take half an hour at weekends. Go before 10:00 or after 15:00, or pick a smaller stall in a side street.

5. Sweet Water Noodles (甜水面)

Heat 1/5 · Barely spicy

Thick, almost pencil-sized noodles coated in a dark brown-sugar sauce with crushed peanuts. The chilli is a background note, not the point of the dish.

  • Sweet first, heat later. Most visitors taste sugar and peanut before they notice any chilli. The heat is low enough that non-spice eaters usually finish the bowl.
  • Chewy, heavy noodles. The noodles are firm and dense, so a small portion fills you up. Mix from the bottom so every strand picks up the syrup.
  • It can be too sweet. This is a sweet dish, not a neutral one. If you dislike sugar in savoury food, skip it and take an egg waffle instead.

6. Douhua (豆花)

Heat 1/5 · Sweet or savoury

Soft tofu pudding, served either chilled and sweet or warm in a savoury broth. The texture sits somewhere between silken tofu and a steamed egg.

  • The sweet version is 0/5. Chilled douhua with fermented rice (酒酿) and a little syrup is sweet, lightly alcoholic and completely chilli-free — a good dessert after a spicy meal.
  • The savoury version can be mild. Meat douhua or pickled-vegetable douhua hotpot is salty and savoury. Ask for 清汤 (clear broth) and no chilli, and it becomes a gentle soup.
  • Texture like steamed egg. It is smooth and slippery, and works as a palate cleanser between heavier dishes rather than as a main course.

7. Chaoshou (抄手)

Heat 0/5 · With clear broth

Sichuan wontons, larger and thicker-skinned than the Cantonese kind, served either in a clear soup or dressed in red oil. The clear-soup version is a staple non-spicy lunch.

  • Clear soup or chicken broth. Order 清汤抄手 (clear-soup wontons) or 鸡汤抄手 (chicken-broth wontons). Both are mild, savoury and chilli-free.
  • Big and generous. Each wonton is a full mouthful, and the filling is meaty rather than stretched with flour. A small bowl is usually enough for lunch.
  • Add heat only if you want it. The red-oil version (红油抄手) is the same dumpling in chilli oil — a useful comparison if you are travelling with someone who does eat spice.

8. Ice Jelly (冰粉)

Heat 0/5 · Cold and sweet

A soft, translucent jelly served in a bowl of brown sugar syrup with toppings. It is the standard finish to a hotpot meal and the most effective chilli extinguisher on this list.

  • The chilli extinguisher. Cold, sweet and watery, it cools the mouth faster than water or beer. Locals order it before they start the hotpot, not after.
  • Hand-rubbed texture. Traditional bingfen is made from rubbed seeds and set into a jelly full of tiny bubbles. It is softer than the powdered version, which can taste rubbery.
  • Toppings do the work. Brown sugar, hawthorn flakes, raisins, sesame and crushed peanuts are standard. Ask for less sugar (少糖) if you prefer it subtle.

How to Order Without Chilli

Chengdu menus are cooked to order, so a few phrases solve most problems. Learn these five and you can eat your way through the city without a single tear.

Say this Pinyin Meaning
不辣bù làNot spicy
不要辣椒bù yào là jiāoNo chilli
清汤qīng tāngClear broth
蘸碟不要辣椒zhàn dié bù yào là jiāoDipping sauce without chilli
少糖shǎo tángLess sugar (for sweet dishes)

A Non-Spicy Food Day in Chengdu

You do not need to avoid hotpot entirely — just build a chilli-free frame around it. This is the sequence locals use when they have visitors who cannot eat heat.

08:00 · BREAKFAST

Egg waffle + soy milk

One cream-and-pork-floss egg waffle, eaten on the way to the panda base. Cheap, warm and no chilli.

12:00 · LUNCH

Clear-soup chaoshou

A bowl of chicken-broth wontons, plus a cold sweet douhua if the shop has it.

15:00 · SNACK

Sweet-skin duck + ice jelly

A quarter duck wrapped for the walk, then a bowl of bingfen in a shaded dessert stall.

18:30 · DINNER

Tiaojiao beef

Clear broth, thin beef, a dipping saucer you mix yourself — mild and filling.

22:30 · LATE NIGHT

Mom's pig-trotter soup

The classic Chengdu night bowl: milky broth, soft trotter, dipping sauce on the side. It closes the day without a trace of chilli.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is all Chengdu food spicy?

No. Chengdu and Sichuan cooking use more than twenty flavour profiles, and only a few are built on chilli. Everyday local food includes clear-soup wontons, pig-trotter soup, sweet-skin duck, egg waffles, sweet water noodles and iced jelly — plenty of it has no chilli at all.

What is the least spicy Chengdu dish?

Mom's pig-trotter soup (老妈蹄花) and tiaojiao beef (跷脚牛肉) are both cooked in clear broth with no chilli. Sweet-skin duck (甜皮鸭) is sweet and savoury. All three are safe for anyone who cannot eat heat.

How do I ask for no chilli in Chengdu?

Say 不辣 (bù là, not spicy) or 不要辣椒 (bù yào là jiāo, no chilli). For soup noodles ask for 清汤 (qīng tāng, clear broth). At a dipping station, say 蘸碟不要辣椒 (no chilli in the dip) — or simply point at the chilli and shake your head.

Is sweet water noodles spicy?

Barely. Sweet water noodles (甜水面) are thick chewy noodles coated in brown-sugar sauce and crushed peanuts, with only a whisper of chilli. Most visitors taste sweetness first. If you dislike sweet food, order something else.

Is douhua sweet or savoury?

Both exist. The sweet version is chilled, often served with fermented rice and syrup; the savoury version comes in a salty broth, sometimes as a douhua hotpot with meat or pickled vegetables. Ask for the non-spicy version of the savoury one.

Where can I find non-spicy food in Chengdu?

Almost every neighbourhood has noodle shops, dessert stalls and clear-soup restaurants. Avoid hotpot and dry-pot restaurants, look for 清汤 (clear broth) on the menu, and use the dessert shops as a reset between spicy meals.

Is ice jelly really made by hand?

Traditional bingfen (冰粉) is made from the seeds of Nicandra physalodes, rubbed by hand and set into a soft jelly full of tiny bubbles. Many stalls now use instant powder, but the hand-rubbed version has a softer, less rubbery texture.

Conclusion

Chengdu's chilli reputation is earned, but it is not a trap. The city eats clear broth in the morning, syrup-glazed duck in the afternoon and iced jelly at midnight. Learn the eight dishes above, keep the phrase 不辣 in your phone, and you can spend a week here without burning your mouth once.
